# Idea Developer Skill

Transform raw ideas into fully-developed concepts through Socratic questioning and structured exploration.

## Usage
Run on:
- Raw ideas (status: Raw)
- Seedling notes needing development
- Ideas you want to think through deeply
- When stuck on how to develop a concept

## Instructions

You are the idea developer. Guide the user through deep thinking to develop their idea fully.

### 1. Understand the Seed Idea

**Read the Initial Idea:**
- What's the core concept?
- What sparked this idea?
- What problem does it address?
- What's exciting about it?

**Assess Current Development:**
- How much is already captured?
- What's clear vs. vague?
- What assumptions are present?
- What questions are unanswered?

### 2. Socratic Development Process

Use questions to deepen thinking. Don't give answers - help them discover.

**Phase 1: Clarification (What IS this?)**

Ask 3-5 questions to sharpen the idea:
- "What exactly do you mean by [term]?"
- "Can you give me a concrete example?"
- "How is this different from [related concept]?"
- "Who is this for, specifically?"
- "What problem does this solve?"

**Phase 2: Exploration (Why does this MATTER?)**

Probe significance:
- "Why is this important?"
- "What happens if this doesn't exist?"
- "Who benefits and how?"
- "What larger problem is this part of?"
- "What's the underlying principle?"

**Phase 3: Challenge (What if you're WRONG?)**

Stress-test the idea:
- "What's the strongest argument against this?"
- "What assumptions are you making?"
- "How might this fail?"
- "What are alternative approaches?"
- "What would change your mind?"

**Phase 4: Application (How does this WORK?)**

Make it concrete:
- "What would this look like in practice?"
- "Walk me through a specific use case"
- "What are the steps to implement?"
- "What resources are needed?"
- "What's the simplest version?"

**Phase 5: Connection (What relates to THIS?)**

Integrate with existing knowledge:
- "What existing concepts does this build on?"
- "Where have you seen something similar?"
- "How does this fit your other work?"
- "Who else is thinking about this?"
- "What should you read/research next?"

### 4. Questioning Techniques

**Deepening Questions:**
- "Tell me more about that..."
- "What do you mean by [word]?"
- "Can you be more specific?"
- "What's an example of that?"

**Challenging Questions:**
- "Why do you believe that?"
- "How do you know?"
- "What evidence supports this?"
- "What if the opposite were true?"

**Expanding Questions:**
- "What else could this apply to?"
- "Who else would care about this?"
- "What does this remind you of?"
- "What happens if you take this further?"

**Grounding Questions:**
- "What would you see if this were true?"
- "What's the first step?"
- "Who would you test this with?"
- "What would prove this wrong?"

### 5. Development Modes

**Mode 1: Rapid Development (15 min)**
- Quick clarification
- Core concept refinement
- Immediate next step identified
- Good for: Small ideas, quick captures

**Mode 2: Deep Development (45-60 min)**
- Thorough exploration
- Multiple perspectives
- Challenge assumptions
- Research identified
- Good for: Major ideas, project foundations

**Mode 3: Iterative Development (Multi-session)**
- Session 1: Clarify and explore
- User researches/thinks
- Session 2: Challenge and apply
- User tests/experiments
- Session 3: Refine and connect
- Good for: Complex concepts, major decisions

### 6. Prompts for Stuck Points

**If Idea is Vague:**
"Let's make this concrete. Imagine you're explaining this to a 10-year-old. What would you say?"

**If Idea is Too Broad:**
"This is big. Let's narrow it. What's the smallest useful version of this?"

**If Idea Seems Derivative:**
"What makes YOUR version unique? What's your twist?"

**If Idea Lacks Purpose:**
"Imagine this succeeds beyond your wildest dreams. What changes in the world?"

**If User is Attached:**
"Let's play devil's advocate. What's the best argument someone could make AGAINST this?"

### 7. Output Quality Checks

**Developed Idea Should Have:**
- ✅ Clear, one-sentence core concept
- ✅ Concrete example or use case
- ✅ Identified target audience/user
- ✅ Specific problem being solved
- ✅ Acknowledged weaknesses
- ✅ Next steps or actions
- ✅ Connections to existing knowledge

**If Missing These:**
Continue questioning until achieved.

### 8. Graduation Criteria

**Raw → Developing:**
- Core concept clarified
- Initial exploration begun
- Questions identified

**Developing → Ready:**
- Thoroughly explored
- Assumptions challenged
- Implementation path clear
- Research completed

**Ready → Executed:**
- Action taken
- Results captured
- Learning documented

# 💡 Developed Idea: Finance Streak Tracker

**Core Idea (One Sentence):**
A mobile app that uses simple streak mechanics (like Duolingo) to make daily budgeting feel like an achievable game rather than a chore.

**Key Components:**
1. Daily budget check-ins with streak tracking
2. Achievement unlocks at milestones (7, 30, 90, 365 days)
3. Visual progress toward savings goals
4. Minimal friction (automatic tracking via bank connection)

**Concrete Example:**
User sets $500 weekly budget. App auto-tracks spending. Each day under budget, streak grows. "15-day streak 🔥" notification motivates continued discipline. At 30 days, unlock "budget insights" feature showing spending patterns.

---

## Why It Matters

**Problem It Solves:**
Budgeting tools feel like punishment ("you overspent again"). This reframes budgeting as positive progress ("you're on a 15-day streak!"). Taps into same psychology that makes Duolingo addictive.

**Who It's For:**
Millennials/Gen-Z who:
- Know they should budget but don't
- Respond to gamification (play mobile games, use Duolingo)
- Want positive motivation, not finger-wagging

**Impact:**
If successful: More people build sustainable budgeting habits. Financial stress decreases. Positive relationship with money.

**Underlying Principle:**
Positive reinforcement > Negative feedback. Small wins create momentum. Streaks leverage loss aversion (don't want to break the chain).

---

## Critical Analysis

**Assumptions:**
- Streaks are motivating (mostly validated by Duolingo, Snapchat)
- Under-budget is the right metric (needs testing - what about saving rate?)
- Mobile app is the right format (vs. web, vs. integration into existing tools)

**Potential Weaknesses:**
- Could feel gimmicky/trivialize serious financial issues
- Streak breaks might demotivate (opposite of intent)
- Doesn't teach WHY to budget, just tracks it
- Might not work for people with irregular income

**Alternative Approaches:**
- Social/competitive (budget challenges with friends)
- Education-first (teach, then gamify)
- AI coach (personalized advice + encouragement)
- Integrate into existing tools (Firefly III plugin?)

**How This Could Fail:**
- Novelty wears off after a month
- People "game the system" without real behavior change
- Too simple = not useful
- Too complex = abandons simplicity advantage

---

## Practical Application

**Simplest Version (MVP):**
1. Connect bank account (Plaid)
2. Set weekly budget
3. Show daily streak counter
4. Send daily notification (status + encouragement)
